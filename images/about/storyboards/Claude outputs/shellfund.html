01

Overview

A personal finance app for college students, designed with AI helping at every step.

Problem

Students deal with uneven income, loans and shared bills, often without much money know-how.

Goal

An app that is helpful, quick to use, never judgmental, private and made for phones first.

Approach

I used ChatGPT, Whyser and Visily for research, ideas and prototypes. I made the design decisions.

The Question

Can AI be a practical design partner that keeps users first?

Ground rules ChatGPT

Avoiding manipulative design

A finance app handles private data and strong feelings, so I asked ChatGPT to argue the other side and list the tricks money apps use on people. I turned each one into a rule for my design.

Data privacy

Avoid hiding how data is used or making it hard to leave.

Do give clear privacy controls.

Honest advice

Avoid ads disguised as advice.

Do show how advice was reached.

Healthy behavior

Avoid guilt, pressure games and comparing users.

Do be supportive, not pushy.

Inclusion

Avoid one-size-fits-all defaults.

Do design for different money situations.

Design Process

From a list of needs and a user flow to a visual style and a first working prototype.

Needs list ChatGPT

What “good” looks like

I turned the research into a plan for the app: made for phones first, with a dashboard, a ShellAI mentor, spending insights, budgets and savings goals. It follows WCAG 2.2 AA, the standard accessibility guidelines. I also set clear targets so success was easy to measure.

Main challenge: students have little attention to spare, so the first session has to show value fast.

Success targets

  • 90%set up a budget in 10 minutes
  • 80%link an account in 5 minutes
  • 60%return weekly after a month

04

Accessibility Redesign

An accessibility check of the first prototype showed who it left out, and how to fix it.

Review ChatGPT

Where the first design fell short

I asked ChatGPT to check the first prototype against WCAG 2.2, then confirmed each finding on the screens myself. Four problems stood out, each with a clear fix.

Takeaway: the most common problem was relying on color alone.

  • 1
    Low contrast

    Faint progress bars and tags.

    Fix: at least 4.5:1 contrast.
  • 2
    Color alone

    Payment status shown only by color.

    Fix: add an icon and text.
  • 3
    Tiny buttons

    Icons under 44×44px and no focus outlines.

    Fix: 44px buttons and visible focus outlines.
  • 4
    Confusing terms

    APR and payoff terms, plus alarming red buttons.

    Fix: short explanations and calmer buttons.
Redesign Visily

Before and after

I used Visily to apply the fixes while keeping the layout and features the same, then finished the details in Figma. Quick actions got bigger buttons and clear labels, and charts now show percentages as well as color.

Final design

The accessible ShellFund

The final version keeps the energy of Bold Momentum while meeting accessibility standards: high-contrast colors, more breathing room, and simpler buttons.

05

Walkthrough & Reflection

Testing the guided onboarding with a team of experts, then looking back at working with AI.

Walkthrough ChatGPT

Walking in a student's shoes

Five people from product, development, UX, marketing and UI walked through one scenario: an overwhelmed freshman choosing a student loan repayment plan, starting with guided onboarding. ChatGPT helped me sum up the notes.

Findings

What we found, and what changed

We ran the walkthrough twice: once from a student's view, and once for the people ShellFund also affects, like financial aid staff and peer mentors. The strongest ideas are below.

For students

  • Loan terms overwhelm freshmenLoan Type Wizard
  • Students may not feel motivated“Why this matters” blocks
  • They fear choosing wrongSafe-to-explore mode

For advisors & staff

  • A person must step in for tricky casesEasy handoff to a person
  • Advisors lose time catching upPre-session summary
  • Staff must trust the numbersShow the math
Key Takeaway

Students need guidance, clarity and reassurance. Staff need easy handoffs, clear numbers and less workload.

Reflection

AI as a design partner

AI made exploring and trying ideas much faster, from understanding the problem to building prototypes. It worked best as a partner: I checked its work and decided what to keep.
