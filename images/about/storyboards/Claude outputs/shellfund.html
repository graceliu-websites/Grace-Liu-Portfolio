01

Overview

A personal finance app for college students, designed with Gen AI as a co-pilot at every stage.

Problem

Students juggle irregular income, loans and shared expenses, often with little financial know-how.

Goal

An app that is helpful, low-friction, non-judgmental, private and built mobile-first.

Approach

ChatGPT, Whyser and Visily supported research, ideation and prototyping, with me making the design calls.

The Question

Can Gen AI work as a practical, user-centered design partner?

Ethical guardrails ChatGPT

Designing against dark patterns

A finance app holds sensitive data and emotions, so I asked ChatGPT to play devil's advocate and list the dark patterns such apps fall into. Each one became a positive guideline for the design.

Data privacy

Avoid hidden data practices and hard exits.

Do give clear, user-controlled privacy.

Honest advice

Avoid disguised or biased recommendations.

Do show how advice is reached.

Healthy behavior

Avoid guilt, forced gamification and comparison.

Do be supportive and non-pressuring.

Inclusion

Avoid exclusionary, one-size-fits-all defaults.

Do design for diverse money situations.

04

Accessibility Redesign

A WCAG 2.2 review of the first prototype showed who it left out, and how to fix it.

Review ChatGPT

Where the first design fell short

I had ChatGPT review the first prototype against WCAG 2.2, then checked each finding on the screens myself. Four risks stood out, each with a clear fix.

Takeaway: the most common failure was relying on color alone.

  • 1
    Low contrast

    Faint progress bars and pill tags.

    Fix: at least 4.5:1 contrast.
  • 2
    Color-only meaning

    Payment status shown by color alone.

    Fix: pair color with an icon and text.
  • 3
    Small tap targets

    Icons under 44×44px and no focus rings.

    Fix: 44px targets and visible focus.
  • 4
    Unexplained jargon

    APR and payoff terms, alarming red buttons.

    Fix: tooltips and calmer buttons.
Redesign Visily

Before and after

I used Visily's prompts to apply the fixes while keeping the layout and features the same, then finished the details in Figma. Quick actions got larger targets and clear labels, and charts now show percentages instead of color alone.

Final design

The accessible ShellFund

The final version keeps the energy of the Bold Momentum direction while meeting WCAG minimums: high-contrast colors, roomier spacing, and simpler buttons.

05

Walkthrough & Reflection

Testing the guided onboarding with experts, then looking back at working with Gen AI.

Cognitive walkthrough ChatGPT

Walking in a student's shoes

Five stakeholders from product, development, UX, marketing and UI walked through one scenario: an overwhelmed freshman who needs to pick a student loan repayment plan, starting with guided onboarding. ChatGPT helped synthesize the notes.

Findings

What we found, and what changed

We ran the walkthrough twice: once for students, and once for the people ShellFund also affects, such as financial aid staff and peer mentors. The strongest ideas are below.

For students

  • Loan terms overwhelm freshmenLoan Type Wizard
  • Motivation can't be assumed“Why this matters” blocks
  • They fear choosing wrongSafe-to-explore mode

For advisors & staff

  • Humans must step in for complex casesHuman handoff triggers
  • Advisors lose time gathering contextPre-session context pack
  • Workers must trust the numbersTransparent calculation view
Key Takeaway

Students need guidance, clarity and reassurance. Workers need handoffs, transparency and less workload.

Reflection

Gen AI as a design partner

Gen AI made exploration and iteration much faster, from framing the problem to rapid prototyping. It worked best as a partner, with me checking and deciding what to keep.
